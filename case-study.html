Alabaster Jar Project

Creating a brand worthy of their impact.

Organization
Alabaster Jar Project
Empowers survivors of human trafficking and sexual exploitation through long-term housing and restorative services, with an emphasis on peer leadership.
Location
San Diego County, CA
Year
2024
Site
alabasterjarproject.org ↗

The challenge

Ten years of quiet, serious work — and a brand that didn’t show it.

After more than a decade of serving survivors, Alabaster Jar Project’s visual brand no longer matched the depth of their impact. They needed a way to reach more donors, invite local volunteers in, and be recognized as the trusted authority they had already become.

The work had to hold two truths at once: the reality of trafficking, and the joy, beauty, and empowerment that are possible on the other side of it.

01 — Brand strategy

Start with the why.

Before anything visual, we sat with the team and the survivors they serve to name what Alabaster Jar is for, who it speaks to, and what it should feel like to encounter. Everything after this points back here.

02 — Visual language

Urgency and hope in the same frame.

A palette, type, and photographic direction that never softens the first truth and never loses sight of the second — so a first-time visitor feels the weight and the way forward at once.

03 — Visual identity

A mark they can carry.

A new logo and system built to live everywhere the organization does — on a survivor’s tote bag as comfortably as on a grant application.

04 — Copywriting

Plain words for hard things.

Voice and messaging across the site and print — direct about what happens to survivors, and just as direct about what becomes possible for them.

05 — Video production

Let them speak for themselves.

A brand film and interview series shot with survivors, staff, and board — the “why” in their own words.

06 — Information architecture

A site that knows who it’s talking to.

Every page mapped before it was designed — separate paths for someone seeking help, someone who wants to give, and someone who wants to volunteer.

07 — Website design, development & motion

Built to be kept up by the people who run it.

Designed, developed, and handed over with the structure and motion in place — so the team can keep telling their story without a designer in the room.
